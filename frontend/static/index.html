Onset criteria momp.stats.detect

"Onset" = the first wet spell of the season. A cell fires if every one of wet_spell consecutive days exceeds wet_init mm/day, and the sum over those days exceeds wet_threshold mm. Optional dry-spell check (dry_spell, dry_threshold, dry_extent): if enabled, a candidate onset is rejected when a dry_spell-day dry period begins within dry_extent days after it — guards against false-onset from pre-monsoon showers that fizzle out.

Changing any of these re-runs detection from scratch against the raw rainfall — first recompute takes a few seconds for deterministic models, up to a minute for 51-member ensembles.

Isochrone overlay

forecast · solid + circles observed · dashed halo
Each color is one DOY. Three visual cues per day, always legible even when fcst and obs coincide exactly: the crisp solid line with open circles is the forecast onset front; the wider, softer dashed halo beneath it is the observed front. Where they agree exactly you'll see the circles riding inside the halo; where they diverge the circles sit in one place and the halo in another — unambiguous. Smaller gap = better spatial agreement.
Background shading is the obs "monsoon has visited" region per DOY: each cell is tinted with the colour of the *first* iso DOY by which obs onset arrived there. Cells visited even later than the latest iso DOY plotted still fall into the last band so every land-and-onset cell gets shaded. A forecast contour is doing well when its crisp line tracks the boundary between its colour band and the next-later one. A forecast line sitting *inside* the band of its colour means the model thinks the front is further south than obs (forecast late); *outside* the band means the model thinks the front is further north (forecast early).
Obs contours can appear as closed loops rather than single south-to-north arcs: this is geographically correct, not a viz bug. Real obs has interior NaN cells (where the detector didn't fire), and the level-set boundary on a finite domain with internal holes is a closed curve. Synthetic forecasts have monotone-in-lat onset fields → their level sets are just horizontal arcs → open lines. Both tell you the same thing about *where the visited region ends*; the visual asymmetry is real-data signal, not noise.
Hausdorff (worst-point) and Fréchet (path-aware) distances per day below. Gray cells are no observed onset within the 1 May–30 Sep window; the dashed contour traces the data/no-data boundary in those regions, not a real DOY transition.
area of disagreement through the season

Progression curve

—
Each model is a line; x-axis is DOY, y is the area of disagreement between forecast and observed "onset-by-day" masks, in 10⁶ km². IOE rises when the forecast front is in the wrong place at that day; it peaks mid-season when the front is moving fastest and collapses to zero once both fields agree "onset everywhere" late in the season. SPS (dotted) is the ensemble generalisation — per-cell Brier score of P(onset ≤ d) against the observed indicator; it's always ≤ IOE for the same data (soft probabilities beat hard indicators). Lower is better. For multi-year ranges the line is the per-day median across years; the shaded band is a 95% bootstrap confidence interval on that median (percentile-method pair bootstrap on the year axis), so non-overlapping bands between two models indicate a statistically distinguishable difference at that day. The dashed vertical line marks the primary model's IOE peak DOY — the day at which the front is most wrong. A late-peaking model lags; an early-peaking model leads, even when their season-integrated totals match. Year-to-year IQR is reported in the JSON for each _q25 / _q75 key but is no longer drawn here, to keep the multi-model chart legible.
probabilistic calibration curve

Reliability

—
BS = MCB − DSC + UNC is a proper-score identity (residual should be ~0). N counts pooled (forecast probability, observation) pairs across cells × years. N_eff corrects for spatial autocorrelation (Dutilleul 1993: n · (1 − I) / (1 + I) where I is the per-year rook-contiguity Moran's I on the observed indicator); take N_eff as the honest independent-sample count when reading confidence statements. N_eff = N would mean zero spatial autocorrelation, which is effectively never true on a contiguous onset field.
per-cell forecast error in days

CRPS field

—
Per-cell CRPS in days, lower is better (0 = perfect). The "no-onset" atom is handled by mapping NaT to a sentinel beyond the season window before the Hersbach ensemble CRPS is evaluated — a proper score for the mixed atom+continuous distribution, related to but not identical with Hemri et al. 2014's analytical censored-Gaussian CRPS. For ≥ 2-member ensembles we use the Ferro 2014 fair correction so the finite-ensemble bias doesn't penalise small ensembles (e.g. IFS-S2S's 11 members) relative to large ones. Single-member (deterministic) forecasts reduce to sentinel-augmented MAE.
geographic bias of the onset region

Displacement + area bias

For each "onset-by-DOY" threshold, we ask two questions of the forecast's onset region vs observation's: (1) how far is the forecast's centroid from obs centroid? (blue, great-circle km on the left axis), and (2) does the forecast cover more or less area than obs? (amber, % on the right axis; positive = forecast region too large, negative = too small). Near-zero on both = spatially unbiased forecast at that threshold. A sustained positive area-bias with small centroid shift means the forecast is "everywhere wet too early" rather than geographically misplaced.
spatial skill vs neighborhood size

Fractions skill vs neighborhood

—
One line per onset-by-DOY threshold. FSS rises with neighborhood as the forecast gets credit for being spatially close; the question is how big a neighborhood it takes to be useful. The faint dotted line per color is that threshold's useful bar (0.5 + 0.5·p); the faint dash-dot is its no-skill bar (FSS = p, what a base-rate-only forecast scores). p is the obs base rate at that DOY threshold.
